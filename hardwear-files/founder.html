Founder

The bloke who kept binning caps.

Ashley Roberts spent fourteen years on the tools in North Wales, and kept a list of how every cap he owned died. HARDWEAR is what came out of being annoyed for long enough.

Ashley Roberts

Fourteen years of ruined hats.

He started at sixteen labouring for a scaffolding firm, and he wore a cap every single day of it. Rain, roof felt, August sun on a flat roof in Rhosddu. Every cap did the same thing: went stiff, went pale round the front, and started to smell by the second month.

The brief he eventually wrote for himself was eight words long. Build it like workwear, line it like sportswear.

He bought cheap ones and expensive ones. The expensive ones lasted about a fortnight longer. Nobody, as far as he could tell, had ever actually designed a cap for a person who sweats for a living.

He is not a designer and he has never pretended to be one. What he had was fourteen years of ruining caps and an annoying habit of writing things down. Somewhere around 2017 the notes turned into an actual spreadsheet: every cap he bought, what it cost, and the week it went wrong. Sixty-odd rows by the end of it. The last column was just called how it died.

"I wasn't trying to start a company. I was trying to stop being annoyed."

Read down that column and the same four answers keep coming back. The sweatband goes. The crown goes soft and stops sitting right. The colour bleaches round the front panel. And the snap or the buckle digs in under a hard hat until you take the thing off, which rather defeats the point.

The other thing the spreadsheet showed was a gap. Every technical cap solved the sweat and looked like a running accessory. Every workwear cap looked right and soaked like a flannel. Nobody was selling the obvious thing — canvas on the outside, because canvas is what makes a work cap read as a work cap, and proper technical fabric on the inside, because that's where the problem actually is.

So he stopped shopping and wrote a specification instead. Two pages, mostly about what it must not do. Then he spent the best part of two years and a lot of unanswered emails finding a manufacturer willing to build it in the quantities a bloke from Wrexham could pay for. Six samples came back in early 2021. He gave five to mates and one to his old foreman.

All six came back with the same report: it didn't get the white ring. That was the whole thing.

How it's made

We don't make them. We're very particular about who does.

There is no HARDWEAR factory and we're not going to pretend otherwise. The cap is built to our specification by a manufacturer who has been making technical headwear for a lot longer than we've existed, and who was willing to take on a first order small enough to be embarrassing.

What we do own is the specification and the standard. Every batch is sampled before it's accepted — sweatband stitch, crown shape, badge alignment, a soak test on the shell. Batches have been sent back. If one gets past us, the guarantee is how you hold us to it.

How it happened

The slow version.

2008
Leaves school at sixteen and starts labouring for a scaffolding firm outside Wrexham. Buys his first work cap the same week.
2015
Seven years in, and the maths is annoying him: four or five caps a year, every year, all of them dead before Christmas.
2017
Starts logging them properly. Every cap he buys, what it cost, and the week it failed. The spreadsheet reaches sixty rows before he admits what it's for.
2019
Writes a two-page specification for the cap nobody was selling — workwear canvas outside, technical fabric inside — and starts emailing manufacturers. Most don't reply.
2021
One of them finally builds it. Six samples go out to five mates and an old foreman. All six come back saying the same thing.
2023
Leaves the tools for good and puts the first production run of 200 on sale. It goes in nine days, mostly to people he'd worked with.
2024
Introduces the 90-day sweat-through guarantee, on the grounds that if the specification is right it shouldn't cost anything.
2026
Over 2,300 reviews and crews wearing them on sites in all three nations. Still one cap. Still one bloke reading every batch report.

What we won't do

Three rules we've written down so you can hold us to them.

01

One product, done properly

There is one cap. When there's a genuine reason for a second one there'll be a second one, and not before. We'd rather improve this than dilute it with a bobble hat nobody asked for.

02

We don't claim what we can't prove

You'll notice this site is light on numbers. Plenty of caps are sold on figures nobody ever tests. Until we hold a report we can show you, we won't print the claim — and when those certificates exist they'll go up here, not into an advert.

03

The guarantee is real

No receipt, no photographs, no form with fourteen fields. If it looks tired inside 90 days we replace it, because arguing with a roofer about a £42 cap is not a business we want to be in.

Where

Run out of Wrexham, and staying that way.

There was no strategic reason to base it here. It's where he's from and where the people he trusts are. The caps are built elsewhere by people who are better at it than we'd be — but every decision about what one is gets made about half a mile from where he grew up.

Judge it by the fourth month.

Anything looks good in week one. Wear it through a summer, then decide — and if it doesn't hold up inside 90 days we'll replace it.
